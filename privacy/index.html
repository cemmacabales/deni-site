Privacy policy

Effective October 11, 2026

Deni keeps everything on your iPhone. It doesn't collect anything about you, and nothing you add to it is sent anywhere.

What Deni keeps, and where

Your medications, their schedules, the doses you take or skip, your supply counts, your journal (mood, symptoms and notes), your first name and your settings are saved in a database inside Deni, on your iPhone. Deni has no account, no server, no analytics, no advertising and no crash reporting. It doesn't use push notifications: reminders are scheduled on your iPhone by iOS itself.

Backups and reports

Deni makes a backup file or a PDF report only when you ask it to, and it goes only where you choose to send it, such as Files, Mail, Messages or AirDrop. What happens to it after that is up to you and the app or service you sent it to.

Deleting your data

  • Settings → Your data → Delete all erases everything Deni has saved, at once.
  • Deleting the Deni app erases it too.
  • Your iPhone's own backups (iCloud or a computer) may include Deni's data, as they do for every app. They follow Apple's settings for your device.

Sharing

Deni doesn't sell, share or give your data to anyone, because it never has it. There are no third parties in Deni.

Children

Deni collects no data from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this ever changes, this page will say so first, with a new effective date. Any change to what Deni collects would also change its App Privacy answers on the App Store.

Questions

Write to deni.app.help@gmail.com.

Deni helps you keep track. It doesn't give medical advice.
